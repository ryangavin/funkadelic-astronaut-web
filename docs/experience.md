# The desk experience and the component library

The interactive desk "experience" is not deployed. It lives in Storybook (`npm run dev` → `/storybook/`, or `npm run storybook` on port 6006) under **Experience / ...**, built from the shared library under **Library / Components** and **Library / Foundations**. This page is the long-form account of how those pieces were built; the public press kit is described in the [README](../README.md).

Where things live:

- `src/experience/` — the experience: `Arrival/`, `Desk/`, `Poster/` (the React port of the old poster, once `src/pages/Home`), `sections/` (Band Dossier, Tour Passes), `experiments/`, `debug/`.
- `src/components/2D` (paper, graphics and layout pieces) and `src/components/3D` (solid desk objects and devices) — Storybook **Library / Components / 2D** and **Library / Components / 3D**.
- `src/behaviors`, `src/foundations`, `src/styles`, `src/geometry` — Storybook **Library / Foundations**: **Behaviors** for interaction and perspective, **Styles** for print and surface treatments, **Layout** for composition primitives.
- `src/content/` — the band's data (links, members, releases, live set, stages), shared with the site.

Storybook is the workshop: it runs with accessibility checks, generated documentation and the MCP addon at `/mcp`. Compositions are written as React and mirrored by stories; there is no in-browser page editor. The Vitest addon runs every story as a browser test in headless Chromium, with each play function as its assertions: `npm run test:stories` from the terminal, or the Testing Module in the Storybook sidebar. See [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) for the test commands.

## Desk lamp

**Library / Components / 3D / Desk Lamp / Articulated** lets you drag the base to move the lamp, use its corner grip to rotate, and drag the shade to bend the two rigid arms without shifting the base. Clicking the shade still switches the light; arrow keys aim it when focused. The same interaction is available in **Library / Foundations / Behaviors / Perspective / Gentle mug**, where the registered bulb and mug shadow follow the moving head. Arm reach is bounded; head height stays fixed at the estimated bulb elevation.

## Experiments

Under `src/experience/experiments/` are pieces still being tried out (Storybook **Experience / Experiments**). **Band Introduction** is three ways of handing someone the band on paper, each built as the real printed thing and measured in 720ths of its width like the components: a **Handbill** (`Handbill.tsx`), a 4¼ x 5½ quarter-sheet of coloured card printed both sides in two inks with the spot colour a hair off register, picked up and turned over on a click; a **Mini Zine** (`MiniZine.tsx`), eight photocopied pages on four leaves that hinge on the spine, turned by clicking the right or left page or with the arrow keys, with the toner heavy and every page's print a little skewed on the sheet. Each takes its content as React (the band's is in `Handbill.band.tsx` and `MiniZine.band.tsx`) and its paper, tilt and timing as props. The one-sheet from the same set has graduated to a component, below.

## Tour pass

The first real React component is the complete pink Olive's tour pass. **Library / Components / 2D / Tour Pass** includes separate editable stories for Olive's (118A Main Street), Nyack Neighborhood Music & Arts Festival, and Saturn Lanes, sharing the same event data as the poster. **Experience / Sections / Tour Passes / Tour Row** contains all three in their established arrangement. The poster places this group with `passesX`, `passesY`, `passesWidth`, and `passesRotation`; the individual pass coordinates are relative to the group. Its editable event fields, portrait, and ticket state are documented in Storybook. Every pass is the same size: the card is a fixed 540 units tall, and the time and complete action button have reserved space. Venue, city and location wrap naturally; their typography shrinks only when the complete details exceed the remaining space, remeasured after resizing or font loading. No event copy is clamped, and short content such as Olive's keeps its original type sizes.

The passes read the same three bookings as the legacy poster's tour board (`TOUR_DATES` in `src/components/2D/TourPass/TourPass.data.ts`): Olive's and the Saturn Lanes bowling alley are sample/fictional records and their ticket actions say so, while the September 26, 2026 Nyack Neighborhood Music & Arts Festival entry is user-verified. Never present placeholder details as announced shows; see [legacy-poster.md](legacy-poster.md#tour) for the original timetable rules.

## Layout blocks, the poster and the desk

Layout blocks live alongside the content blocks. **Paper sheet** is the page container: the site's wheat stock inside a dark ink surround, with the torn-paper edging from `src/styles/torn-edge.css`, which any positioned element can reuse by adding the `torn-edge` class. Its print wear stays on the stock, under whatever is placed on the sheet. An optional `imageSrc` prints a picture onto the stock underneath the content, multiplied like ink the way the festival sketch sits behind the poster's hero; `imageSize`, `imagePosition`, `imageOpacity`, and `imageContrast` set the crop and fade (defaults match the hero treatment).

**Stage** (`src/components/2D/Stage/Stage.tsx`) is the page container: everything inside is laid out at a fixed design width, 1440 pixels, and the whole thing is zoomed to the width it is shown at, so the composition keeps every placement and proportion and simply gets bigger on a wide monitor and smaller on a laptop. Zoom is part of layout, so the page's height follows, pixel-sized pieces (Wordmark, Ribbon, SocialIcon) and sheet-unit pieces (Pin, Polaroid, Folder) scale together, and one design pixel is one sheet unit. `minScale` stops the shrinking at a floor, below which the page scrolls sideways, and `maxScale` caps the growth. **Library / Foundations / Layout / Stage** shows a ruler sheet, a capped stage and a floored one at phone width. A `height` fixes the design height too, for a page that is one canvas of set proportions.

**Experience / Poster** (`src/experience/Poster/`, once `src/pages/Home/Home.tsx`) is the old home page as one such canvas: an 11 x 17 poster, 1440 by 2225 design pixels, with the festival map (`assets/festival-map.webp`) printed edge to edge on a single sheet, and the wordmark, the astronaut, the streaming links, the band dossier and the footer links pinned at fixed positions on it, with no seams between sections; every pin's coordinates are in `HOME_LAYOUT` and are also props, so the story's controls have a slider for each. Its stories show it at the design width, on a 1280 laptop, a 1920 monitor, a tablet and a phone.

**Experience / Desk** (`src/experience/Desk/PromoterDesk.tsx`) is an experiment in landing somewhere else: the desk of someone at the Mission Control label who is producing the Nyack festival, a 16 x 9 frame (1440 by 810) with a margin round it, never taller than the window (the page story stands it in a dark room; the docs show just the frame), with everything on it its real size against one reference, the Walkman, 112 mm across and 224 units, so the frame is 720 by 405 mm of desktop, a letter folder is four Walkmans wide and a cassette is a cassette (`REAL_WIDTHS` lists every width in millimetres, `SIZES` the same in units); an open letter folder is 306 mm tall, which is what sets the scale. The band's press package lies closed on the wood, FUNKADELIC / ASTRONAUT on two strips stuck to its cover beside the streaming stickers, a typed label and a sticky note, with the label's own things on and around it: the Walkman lying on the folder with the demo in it, the Handheld with the live set on the disc, the draft running order for September 26 on Mission Control letterhead half under the folder with the band's slot ringed in pen, the festival's site plan (the poster's map, run off on a letter sheet) under that, a mug, a spare tape, pens, a pick, the desk clock keeping real time, the Newton's cradle, the social stickers stuck to the wood along the front edge, and the lamp clamped to the back edge, only its shade in frame, throwing its light across the middle of the desk: click the shade and the room goes dim.

The closed folder is one button: click it and the cover swings open on the label's stamp, the things on it are shoved aside to their open places, and what was inside spills out on a Spill, the prints, the one-sheet, the handbill and the ticket over the cover, the members' three cards, the festival pass and the zine over the well, each the real component to pick up; the tab closes it and draws everything back. Closed, the folder's box is still twice the folder anyone can see — the other half of it is the well, face down on bare desk — so while it is closed that box catches nothing and only the button and the links stuck to the cover do, and the running order and the site plan lying under it stay there to be picked up. Everything on the desk is a Movable: drag it anywhere, and the thing picked up comes to the top, so things can lie over each other and still be found; what the visitor has moved stays put. The closed and open places are in `DESK_LAYOUT`, and `onArrange` reports where everything lies whenever something is put down, which the story logs as an action.

The rest of the desk work — the dossier (**Experience / Desk Dossier**), the perspective desk (**Experience / Perspective Desk**), settings (**Experience / Desk Settings**), the arrival (**Experience / Arrival**) and the debug benches (**Experience / Debug / ...**, `src/experience/debug/`) — is documented in the other files in this folder: [desk settings](desk-settings.md), [dossier arrangements](dossier-arrangements.md), [room controls](room-controls.md), [physical dimensions](desk-physical-dimensions.md), [material scale](desk-material-scale.md) and the [MVP issue list](desk-mvp-issues.md).

**Ribbon** (`src/components/2D/Ribbon/Ribbon.tsx`) is a block topped by one of the site's wavy seams: colour, paper and ink strokes along the same cosine wave the legacy poster's `app.js` draws, with the block's own fill clipped to the wave so the paper above shows through the crests. Its defaults are the poster footer's purple ribbon; frequency, amplitude, tilt, and x/y shift use the same units as the old ribbon studio.

**One-Sheet** (`src/components/2D/OneSheet/OneSheet.tsx`) is a press one-sheet: a letter page Z-folded in three, measured in 720ths of its width, whose lower panels hang from the creases. Folded, the creases spring a little so the bottom panel peeks out beneath the tilted top one, and a touch more under the pointer; a click pulls it open in one motion with both creases opening together so the bottom panel slides out face up, and folds it back the way a hand does, the bottom panel flipped onto the middle and the middle tucked behind the top. The copy is set in a book serif with the letterhead in the logo face, a photograph can spill across a crease, and the sheet casts one shadow that follows the fold. The band's own is `bandOneSheet.tsx` beside the Band Dossier in `src/experience/sections/BandDossier/`, and the **Band Dossier** files it folded in the well beneath the members' pile, where the band's index-card packet used to be.

**Pin** places whatever it wraps at x, y, width, and rotation values measured in sheet units, where the sheet is 1440 units wide, so a composition scales uniformly with the page instead of reflowing.

**Desk** (`src/components/3D/Desk/Desk.tsx`) is the other surface: a wooden desktop seen from a little above, 1440 units across with a `height` in the same units (810 by default, a 16 x 9 frame), so Pins work on it the way they do on a sheet. The figure is drawn, not photographed: the slab (or each of the `boards`, if it is glued up) carries broad bands of tone that a turbulence filter bends into figure, pores lie over that, the room's `light` falls across the satin finish from the upper left, and along the bottom is the desk's rounded front `edge`; the timbers are walnut, oak, ebony and cherry. **Library / Foundations / Layout / Desk** shows the bare top, a few things pinned on it on a Stage, a glued-up top, and the four woods.

## Typography

Typography is shared through `src/styles/fonts.css`: Balsamiq Sans is the body token, Modak is the display token, and Caveat is the handwritten token used by the tour pass. These locally bundled font faces are loaded by Storybook without importing the legacy poster's global CSS.

## Paper and print components

**Astronaut** (`src/components/2D/Astronaut/Astronaut.tsx`) is the complete printed astronaut cutout, including its aged stock, torn silhouette, exposed fibers, and shadow. It composes `PaperSheet` with an optional `shape` (path, reference width/height, and paper margin); shaped sheets use their own aspect ratio and omit the rectangular surround. Size it through its parent or a `Pin`. **Library / Components / 2D / Astronaut Cutout** includes the original, tilted and white-stock variants, multiple sizes/instances, and a Paper Sheet composition. The legacy poster continues to use its own rendering. The component has no automatic motion; its `rotation` prop tilts the whole cutout.

**Weathered** (`src/behaviors/Weathered/Weathered.tsx`) makes a surface worn: patina, fibre flecks, grain and print wear as layers at `z-index: -1` inside the element's own stacking context, so they sit above its background and beneath everything placed on it. A weathered sheet stays worn while the cards laid on it are clean unless they are weathered themselves. Each texture is a prop, `true` or an opacity, and `tone="light"` switches to light flecks screened onto dark stock. The paper sheet, the index card and the folder's leaves all use it.

**Jitter** (`src/behaviors/Jitter/Jitter.tsx`) composes around visual components: `<Jitter preset="cutout"><Astronaut rotation={-6} /></Jitter>`. The `cutout` and `print` presets match the existing poster strengths; both default to 150 ms steps. Optional `x`/`y` overrides are maximum pixel displacement, `rotation` is maximum angular displacement, and `cadenceMs` controls the interval (lower is faster, minimum 16 ms). Use `activation="hover-focus"` for pointer hover or keyboard-visible focus on the wrapper or a descendant; otherwise motion is continuous. `enabled={false}` resets it. Put static positioning and tilt on the wrapper or child: the separate motion layer preserves them. Use a real link/button for interactive content; the wrapper does not add a tab stop by default. Motion stops and resets for reduced-motion preferences, hidden pages, inactive interactions, and unmounting. Each instance updates its own DOM layer without per-frame React renders. The underlying `attachJitter` also takes an array of layers and moves each one independently on the same cadence, which is how Wordmark jitters its letters. **Library / Foundations / Behaviors / Jitter** includes tunable labels, independent instances, and an Animated Astronaut story. This does not migrate the legacy poster's motion.

**PaperStrip** (`src/components/2D/PaperStrip/PaperStrip.tsx`) wraps arbitrary content in a pale paper scrap. It composes `PaperSheet`/`ShapedPaper`, measures the content to fit its backing, and exposes stock, horizontal/vertical padding, and static rotation. Text wraps within the available parent width. The `pale` stock uses the legacy scrap aging colors. The surface shares its torn edges, fibers, speckles, and shadow with the existing shaped paper implementation.

**Wordmark** (`src/components/2D/Wordmark/Wordmark.tsx`) prints arbitrary `children` on one PaperStrip. Use plain text, composed markup, or supplied SVG content; control the ink color, font size, letter spacing, outline, shadow, paper stock, padding, and static tilt. Content is real accessible markup, and naturally wraps within its parent. HTML lettering uses adjusted Modak ascent/descent metrics and compensates for the downward ink shadow so the visible letters sit centrally on the paper; supplied SVG artwork keeps its own explicit baseline and original font metrics. For example: `<Wordmark inkColor="#639ec8">LIVE TONIGHT</Wordmark>`. Motion of the whole strip stays outside: `<Jitter preset="print"><Wordmark>GOOD MUSIC</Wordmark></Jitter>`. Motion of the individual letters is the `jitter` prop: `true` uses the `print` preset, or pass Jitter options (`preset`, `x`, `y`, `rotation`, `cadenceMs`, `activation`, `enabled`). Every character, including text inside supplied markup, becomes its own glyph displaced independently on one shared cadence, like misregistered print; words never break between letters, each text run keeps a visually hidden copy so assistive technology reads it as words rather than letters, and supplied `svg`/`img` artwork moves as one print rather than splitting. Hover/focus activation watches the whole strip, so a link or button inside the content works. Jittered letters are inline blocks, which drops font kerning between them while the prop is on. The reusable component contains no band name or two-line layout. **Library / Components / 2D / Wordmark → Hero Reference** composes two generic instances to reproduce FUNKADELIC / ASTRONAUT; the words, custom SVG lettering geometry, and arrangement live only in that story. Other stories demonstrate editable text, mixed icon/text content, multiple widths/instances, and jittered letters, continuous and on hover/focus of a link inside the strip.

**AdmissionTicket** (`src/components/2D/AdmissionTicket/AdmissionTicket.tsx`) is the hand-cut general-admission ticket from the live section heading. Every printed field is a prop (presenter, title, subtitle, season, location, detail, stub section/stamp/serial, rail copy, price and price serial), plus `rotation` and a `color` for the rail, stamp and price ink. The stock, fibers, age wash and worn-ink filter are scoped per instance so many tickets can share a page. **Library / Components / 2D / Admission Ticket** covers the site heading, a single-show variant, tilt, small size, inks and a fanned stack.

**SocialIcon** (`src/components/2D/SocialIcon/SocialIcon.tsx`) is a platform mark printed like the wordmark: a black silhouette underneath, the glyph in ink on top with a black stroke tucked under its fill, a hard offset shadow, and the worn-ink texture. Marks live in `platforms.ts` (Instagram, Facebook, Bandsintown, Apple Music, Spotify, YouTube, Deezer, Bandcamp). Props are `platform`, `ink` (named site ink or any colour), `size`, `worn` and `label`.

**Polaroid** (`src/components/2D/Polaroid/Polaroid.tsx`) is an instant print: a smooth coated card with a recessed photo window and the thick bottom border you write on, in the classic square pack or the wide landscape pack. The photo is cropped to a `focus`, and `fade` sets how far the chemistry has gone, from true blacks to lifted, vignetted, cyan-shadowed dye. `caption` and `note` are marker handwriting on the border, `tape` adds a masking-tape strip, and `rotation` tilts the print. A `video` plays muted and looping in the window as a preview, with `src` as its poster: a video file, or a YouTube link, which embeds the privacy-enhanced player sized to cover the window like a photo, starting muted and looping, with YouTube's title bar and logo cropped away above and below the window and the player laid out at twice the size and scaled back down so its buttons show at half size: a click on the picture plays or pauses, hovering shows only a small centre play button, and the keyboard shortcuts work (the helpers in `embed.ts` are unit-tested). `plain` shows any picture as supplied with no film treatment. **Library / Components / 2D / Polaroid** covers the member print, the wide pack, fresh and faded prints, the three members fanned, and a print pinned over the festival sketch.

**IndexCard** (`src/components/2D/IndexCard/IndexCard.tsx`) is a ruled 4 x 6 or 3 x 5 card: ivory stock, a red head rule and blue feint lines in 720ths of the width, typed `title`, `subtitle` and body paragraphs that sit on the rules, pen `notes` placed by percentage in the handwritten face, an optional rubber `stamp` in any corner or beside the signature as a sign-off, a `signature` that is either a scrawl (SVG path data, the members' own in `src/experience/sections/BandDossier/signatures.ts`) or a legible hand (Homemade Apple, Kristi and Herr Von Muellerhoff are bundled and exported from `fonts.css`; **Library / Foundations / Styles / Signatures** shows both), an `aside` column hung from the right margin, and a `clearance` that wraps the writing around something clipped over a corner.

**Folder** (`src/components/2D/Folder/Folder.tsx`) is a manila folder lying open, 1440 units across: the back leaf carries the tab `label`, cut along the top or, with `tab="side"`, down the right edge, and the well (`children`), the front `cover` hinges on the spine and swings closed when `open` is false, with `stamps` inside and a `sticker` outside. Stocks are manila, kraft and green.

**Packet** (`src/components/2D/Packet/Packet.tsx`) clips a `Polaroid` over one corner of an `IndexCard` with a gem clip whose outer leg slips behind the card, the card's writing keeping clear of the print; `facts` are ticked off in red pen after the bio.

**Sticker** (`src/components/2D/Sticker/Sticker.tsx`) is a die-cut vinyl sticker: whatever it wraps is the print, a black `keyline` is printed round it, the vinyl (`stock`) is cut a `border`'s width outside the print's outline (`shape`, in artwork units; the default is a millimetre or two on a 40 mm sticker), a clear laminate with one soft sheen lies over the whole face, border included, and it is stuck flat, so it casts almost no shadow beyond the hairline of its own thickness; with `backing` it is still on its glossy release liner, a sheet of backing paper it is kiss-cut on, lying loose on the desk, and the liner stays down when the corner peels: the sticker is what lifts. One corner can `peel`: that corner is off the surface and turned over, its mirror image lying inside the fold underside up, and with an `href` the sticker is a link whose corner curls further under the pointer or on focus. **SocialSticker** (`SocialSticker.tsx`) prints a platform's mark from `SocialIcon` on one, cut around the mark's own silhouette, in the platform's own colour by default (`ink="brand"`, from `platforms.ts`, each muted toward the poster's inks so it sits with the desk, Instagram with its gradient muted the same way) and as a clean flat job: `SocialIcon` now takes `print="flat"`, the silhouette in ink with its counters showing paper through and none of the poster's black, shadow or wear, which stays available as `print="cutout"`.

## Behaviors

**Stack** (`src/behaviors/Stack/Stack.tsx`) piles packets by depth and sifts them when `index` changes: next lifts the top packet, swings it out and drops it under the pile; previous pulls the wanted one out from under and lands it on top, the clipped print lagging the card by a beat. The deeper packets are staggered upward a head row each so every name shows above the card in front, which is the cue to click; with `onSelect` the pile is the control: click a peeking packet to bring it up, the top one to send it under, each packet a labelled button for keyboard and screen-reader use. The pure maths in `sift.ts` is unit-tested; reduced motion snaps instead of flying. **Library / Foundations / Behaviors / Stack** demonstrates the pile on its own.

**Movable** (`src/behaviors/Movable/Movable.tsx`) is a thing on a surface that can be picked up: placed by its corner in the surface's units like a Pin, it slides when its place changes, and with `onMove` it can be dragged by its body, lifting a little and following the pointer, whose travel is scaled to units through the `MovableScale` context (a surface on a Stage is zoomed); a press that starts on a control inside it is left to the control, or with `grab="anywhere"` only sliders and frames are, so a sheet whose whole face is a button can still be picked up, and a drag never ends in a click. Dragged with Alt held, or by the grip that shows at its corner under the pointer, it turns about its centre to face the pointer instead. It takes focus itself so the arrow keys move it and the bracket keys turn it, and `onGrab` lets whoever owns the surface bring it to the top; `onMove` reports the place, with the tilt when it has been turned. A lifted thing throws its own shadow: the lift is a `drop-shadow`, so a round mug casts a round one and a pen a long thin one, rather than the box's. **Library / Foundations / Behaviors / Movable** shows four things on a sheet.

**Spill** (`src/behaviors/Spill/Spill.tsx`) is loose things kept in a container that fall out when it is opened: while `open` is false every `Spilled` item lies packed on the `from` point (or the container's centre), turned every which way and hidden once it has settled; when it turns true each slides out to its own `x`, `y` and `rotation`, lifting off the surface on the way and landing flat, one after another in `order`, after a `delay` for whatever closes over them to get out of the way, and closing draws them back. Each item is a Movable, so it can be dragged once it is out. The container takes no room and the items are placed in the same units as a Pin (`unit` overrides that), so a Spill can sit in a folder's well; the Band Dossier takes `children` for exactly that. **Library / Foundations / Behaviors / Spill** shows three prints and a note falling out of a folder.

## Devices

**Walkman** (`src/components/3D/Walkman/Walkman.tsx`) is a pocket cassette player in 720ths of the width, in silver, blue or black, with a working audio player underneath: `src` is the tape, any audio file the browser can play. The panel is a segmented display: the `title` (or the file's name) spelled out on ten fourteen-segment cells that crawl while the tape plays, a seven-segment counter with a blinking colon, the transport flags and the `side` letter, unlit segments ghosted like a real LCD. The glyph table, the scrolling window and the counter live in `segments.ts` and are unit-tested. The cassette turns behind the door: the hubs spin at play speed, faster when winding and backwards on rewind, the tape packs shift from the left reel to the right as the track plays, and `label` is handwritten on the paper. Piano keys work the transport: PLAY latches down, STOP releases it, and REW and FF wind at eight times speed while held, by pointer or keyboard, or jump ten seconds on a bare click; a serrated wheel on the right edge is the volume. Without a `src` the display reads NO TAPE and the keys are disabled, and what the transport is doing is announced for assistive technology. **Library / Components / 3D / Walkman** shows a tape in the deck, no tape, side B, the three finishes, and the player on the desk. The demo tape in `assets/audio` is a synthesised bass loop standing in until a real track goes in.

**Handheld** (`src/components/3D/Handheld/Handheld.tsx`) is a pocket games console in the proportions of the classic widescreen handheld, 170 by 74 millimetres, in 720ths of the width, in black, silver or white: a glossy plate carries the 4.3 inch 30:17 screen and the strip of small keys beneath it, the directional pad sits over the analog nub on the left, the four face buttons on the right, and the shoulder buttons show over the top edge. The screen plays the disc: `video` is a YouTube link, played through the embedded player and worked over postMessage the way YouTube's own IFrame API does (the wire, the clock and the volume steps live in `player.ts` and are unit-tested), or a video file in a plain video element. It starts muted like a preview so the picture can start on its own; Cross and Start play and pause, Circle stops, the pad steps ten seconds and works the volume, the shoulders skip thirty, Sound turns the volume on, Display steps the brightness, Home puts the idle wave back, Square sets repeat, and Select or Triangle show the readout along the bottom of the picture with the `title` (or what YouTube reports), the flags and the clock. Without a disc the screen idles on its wave and the keys wait; what the transport is doing is announced for assistive technology. **Library / Components / 3D / Handheld** shows the live set on the disc, no disc, the silver one, the three finishes, and the console on the desk. YouTube pauses one embedded player when another starts, so only one of the three finishes plays at a time.

## Small desk objects

The small things a desk has on it, each drawn to the real object and sized by its parent:

- **Mug** (`src/components/3D/Mug/Mug.tsx`) is a coffee mug straight from above, the rim of the `glaze`, the inside wall shading away from the window, the surface of the `drink` with a reflection and a few bubbles at the edge, and the handle turned to `rotation`; `coffee` is how full it is, and empty it shows the bottom and what dried on it.
- **CoffeeRing** (`CoffeeRing.tsx`) is the ring a wet mug leaves, multiplied onto whatever it is laid on, with `masks` cutting out whatever was lying over that surface at the time. The trail of them is in `trail.ts`: coffee runs down the outside of a mug and gathers under its base, so the ring goes down as the mug is set down and is under it the whole time it stands there — lifting the mug only shows what was already on the desk. `useCoffeeTrail` keeps them, taking where the mug lies and what it can be stood on (`Surface`s in desk units, underneath first); each setting down wets everything the base is over and dries every ring already down a shade further, and a ring leaves only by drying out, which takes a dozen or so journeys, so the desk keeps as many as it is given. A mug set half on a sheet of paper leaves half a ring on the sheet and half on the wood, and because the wood's half is cut where the sheet covered it, pulling the sheet out from under the mug takes its half away and leaves the mug standing on the crescent. `Stained` (`Stained.tsx`) wraps anything that has been stood on, laying its rings on its face and clipping them to its edges so they go where it goes; the wood's own are laid on it with `CoffeeRings`. **Library / Components / 3D / Mug → Rings** is a desk with a mug, a sheet of paper, a note and a marker on it to try it with.
- **Pen** (`src/components/3D/Pen/Pen.tsx`) is a ballpoint with the ink tube showing through its clear barrel, a permanent marker or a sharpened pencil, 720 units long with `ink` and `rotation`.
- **StickyNote** (`src/components/2D/StickyNote/StickyNote.tsx`) is a three-inch square note stuck along its top edge, so the bottom lifts and one corner has curled over (`curl`), written in the handwritten face at `size` in 720ths of its width, in five pad `color`s.
- **GuitarPick** (`src/components/2D/GuitarPick/GuitarPick.tsx`) is the standard teardrop with a mark printed on the face.
- **DeskClock** (`src/components/3D/DeskClock/DeskClock.tsx`) is the wedge LCD desk clock, 90 by 60 mm, seen from above: the top of the case with its buttons and the face leaning up toward the desk, the hours and minutes in the Walkman's seven-segment digits with the colon blinking the seconds, the meridian flag and the day and date on a smaller row; it keeps real time (`now` and `running` hold it still for a story), in black, silver or white.

## Foundations

Foundations sit under `src/foundations` and Storybook's **Library / Foundations** section.

- **Cutout Ink** (`src/styles/cutout-ink.css`) is the wordmark's fill-stroke-shadow treatment as one stylesheet with `--cutout-ink`, `--cutout-shadow-x/y` and `--cutout-stroke`; `.cutout-ink--text` applies it to type and `.cutout-ink--svg` with `.cutout-ink__base`/`.cutout-ink__fill` applies it to artwork.
- **Distressed** (`src/foundations/Distressed/Distressed.tsx`) is the ink-on-paper texture: wrap anything in `<Distressed>` for the worn letterpress finish, or use the exported `PrintInkFilter` inside an SVG's defs.
- **Inkjet** (`src/foundations/Inkjet/Inkjet.tsx`) is the other press: what an office machine does to a file on its way to plain copy paper. Whatever it wraps is the file that was sent; what comes back is ink, and every step of the filter is one of the machine's own limits — four inks that reach nowhere near the screen's colours (`gamut`) and a black that dries a warm dark grey, ink that spreads a hair into the fibre (`spread`), a dither of dots in place of continuous tone (`dots`, `dither`), and the faint bands the head leaves where one pass meets the next (`banding`), with a `seed` because no two sheets come out alike. The dots and the bands are composited so they fall away as the ink does, so where the printer lays none the paper is left alone; and since the white of a print is the paper rather than a fifth ink, the sheet is multiplied onto what it lies on and can never be lighter than the stock — printed onto a weathered page, the grain and the fibre show through the picture. The exported `InkjetFilter` goes in an SVG's defs for artwork already inside one. The festival's site plan on the promoter's desk is run off this way, and **Library / Foundations / Styles / Inkjet** stands the file beside the print.
