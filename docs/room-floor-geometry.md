# Floor geometry

Room backgrounds include four 50 mm square wooden legs, inset 65 mm from tabletop edges. Each runs from the floor to the tabletop underside (desk height minus edge thickness). Small desk dimensions bound the inset and thickness. The diagram uses the same leg geometry, including the underside endpoints.

ScaleBench supplies layered floor artwork: a 320 mm tall terracotta pot with a 950 mm plant at x −1200 mm/y 280 mm, and a 360 mm tall, 290 mm diameter wastebasket at x 1200 mm/y 155 mm. The bin rim clears the wall by 10 mm; the plant canopy stays at least 20 mm in front of the wall. Their positions and dimensions stay fixed as the camera, lens, desk or background coverage changes. Other scenes do not receive these props.

`RoomFloorSurface` re-expresses the existing eye relative to the floor and compensates framing to retain the same focal length and principal point. Ordinary `Perspective` and `Solid` artwork then works on the floor exactly as it does on the desktop. Wastebasket uses layered base/contact, shaded cylindrical side, rim and inner-wall artwork, following the Mug construction; it contains no reference mesh, coffee or stains. The plant uses the same pot construction and separate elevated leaf illustrations. Desk legs retain their existing geometry.

Floor artwork is split around the accepted tabletop height. Lower portions paint behind the desktop; raised leaf planes and the upper parts of vessels paint in front. Vertical stems and cylinder sides split at the same projected height, preventing lines through the tabletop. At 40° pitch and 1600 mm wall distance, the legs and floor contacts are visible; at 74.5° pitch much of the support structure is hidden by the top. Narrow shots may crop lateral floor objects. Artwork uses contact shading and fixed highlights, without introducing a new lamp-shadow system. As with other Solid illustrations, the layers approximate volume; props do not participate in collision detection, so unusually enlarged desks may intersect their fixed positions.
